I've been pondering something lately: loving anger.

Sometimes when someone is angry with us, we assume they hate us. Or that they're jealous of us, or that they've been holding something against us. It really does feel that way. And sometimes it's even true, because we are human, and we do feel jealousy, and we do get angry because of it. Often our reasons aren't even good ones.

But that's not always the case.

## Israel and the almost

I was thinking about how God was angry with Israel. So angry that he almost devoured them. Almost wiped them all out. And I wonder about the people standing in that moment. Maybe not all of them, but some must have been thinking, *why is God doing this to us?*

I know that feeling. I feel it often. *Why am I going through all this trouble? God must be angry with me.*

## Anger that corrects

Here is what I'm slowly learning: God's anger toward us is a loving anger. It is how he disciplines us, corrects us, and takes us back to the right path. It is his way of teaching us his ways. "The Lord disciplines the one he loves" (Hebrews 12:6).

It takes time to see that, and I won't pretend otherwise. When you're in the middle of it, it is difficult. It's not easy to say, "Oh, this is just God's loving discipline." Our reasoning fails. Our imagination reaches its limit. All our logic finds no way forward.

So we turn to God and ask, *why? Why are you angry with me?* We start questioning him. We start fighting with him. We pour our own anger out on him.

And then, slowly, the Holy Spirit opens our eyes to see where we were actually heading.

## Love and anger, side by side

I've been in a place like this myself. I was so angry at her. So angry. But it wasn't that I hated her. It was *because* I loved her, and what happened hurt so much. I didn't hate her. I hated the things that happened.

It took time to come out of that, to become a little less angry. It took time to pull the love and the anger apart and see them for what they were.

That's how I want to understand God's anger too. When we love someone, we want to see them do well. We want to see them walking a good path, living a good life. But our human love often has a selfish side. We want the people we love to always make us feel good, or to always go the way we think is right.

God's love is different. His way of working is different.

## Thankful

So I just wanted to write this down. God disciplines us in his loving anger. Not sometimes, but very often. And we should be thankful for it. It is gentle, it is kind, and it is the way of a Father who wants to see us grow.

Let me pray.

> Father God, thank you for your loving anger. Thank you for disciplining us, and for choosing to do it so many times.
>
> I commit my life, my days, my future, and everyone I love into your hands. Discipline me, and every one of us, in your loving way, in your loving anger, whenever it is needed.
>
> Thank you. I'm even laughing as I say it, but I am truly thankful.
