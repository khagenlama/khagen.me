Whenever we hear the word *idolatry*, most of us picture something physical: a statue, a shrine, a temple. Here in Nepal, that is especially true. The word itself can even sound offensive in our cultural context.

But the more I learn about it from the biblical perspective, the deeper it gets. Idolatry is not something that stays visible on the outside. It lives inside us. In today's class, I learned that it is the root of every other sin, and that picture stayed with me. Think of idolatry as a root, and all the other sins as the fruit growing out of it.

That is a profound thing to sit with. It made me look at my own life.

## My own story

There was a time when I desperately wanted something. It overwhelmed me. It took all my attention, all my energy, all my joy. It felt like this was the only thing that could give my life meaning, and that without it, life would be empty and joyless.

So I tried to get it with everything I had. When that didn't work, I tried something else. I tried influencing, using whatever means or connections I had. Still, it didn't come.

Looking back, I can see the pattern:

- It began as a simple **desire**.
- The desire turned into a **demand**.
- When the demand wasn't met, the desperation grew, and it became a **need**.
- Eventually that need became a part of me.

It sounds so simple written like this. But it was not. It is a long journey, and life leads us through these paths in ways we don't expect. Along the way we learn about our lives, about God, and about how to relate to him.

## The heart is quick to idolize

Sometimes I think it's good that I experienced this. I thank God for it. Not sometimes, always. Even though it was not easy.

And I want to say to anyone reading: we don't always do what is right or good. We fall short, even when we want to do good. Sometimes our mind, our heart and our body are not working together, and we find ourselves in conflict with ourselves. That is difficult.

It also shows how fragile the human heart is. It is so easy for us to idolize something. It is soft, and it slips in quietly. We don't notice until it becomes obvious, until it has taken over every part of us. Then we realize it, and we can't do anything about it.

## Where God steps in

When we can't free ourselves, we cry out to God and ask for help. And in his mercy and compassion, he turns to us and rescues us from that bondage.

I wouldn't say it is the only way, but even through those dark days, God does something amazing. He turns the evil into good. That is the truth, and it's what I can say now.

But I'll be honest: while you are in the dark days, it's hard to say. We can say it with our mind, but it doesn't reach the heart. The heart is a completely different dimension of ourselves.

## If you're in it right now

If you are going through any of these stages, lean on God completely. Look to Jesus. The Spirit of Christ will come upon you and lead you through, whatever the situation is. He will strengthen you, guide you, and show you his way. He will free you from every chain, and then we will know true freedom, and we will worship him out of that freedom and love.

Thank you for reading!
